### Individual Profile Pages (hosted on this site)

**Radana Molnarova** — MSc Student (2018–Present), supervised by Prof. L. Rowe
> I am primarily interested in the dynamics of immune investment. Immunity is undoubtedly an important trait with strong ties to individual survival.

Studies sex differences in immunity within populations of the bean beetle *Callosobruchus maculatus*, focusing on how females invest differently in immune parameters relative to mating-related harm from males. Plans to experimentally induce infection to examine consequences of differential immune investment.
Contact: radana.molnarova@mail.utoronto.ca

**Katja Kasimatis** — Postdoc with Cutter and Rowe labs (2020–present)
> My research explores the interplay of sex-specific selection and sexual conflict throughout the life cycle to understand how sexual dimorphism arises across the genotype-phenotype map and how sex differences relate to fitness.

Also studies "how the autosomal genome responds to sex-specific selection, what is the genetic architecture of sexually dimorphic traits, and the extent to which sexual conflict truly drives genome evolution" using the *Caenorhabditis* nematode model system.
Contact: k.kasimatis@utoronto.ca

**Rowan French** — PhD Student, with Mahler and Rowe (2019–present)
Studies "patterns of morphological diversity and the evolutionary drivers of phenotypic divergence," with particular focus on sexually dimorphic traits, concentrating on "the evolution of sexual dimorphism and reproductive variation in longhorned beetles (Coleoptera: Cerambycidae)." Investigates "the extent to which sexual dimorphism covaries with mating behaviours, larval and adult ecologies, and pheromone signal divergence" using phylogenetic comparative approaches and single-species studies, and "trade-offs between exaggerated secondary sexual structures and other costly traits, including brain size and locomotion."
